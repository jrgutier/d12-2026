What the room got wrong

Dollars paid vs. model value, by position

In a superflex with 24 quarterbacks starting every week, the QB replacement level sits around QB24 — roughly 198 points, higher than any running back outside the top eight. The room priced quarterbacks like a one-QB league and running backs like a 2019 draft. That gap is the league's central mispricing, and it is worth about ten points a week to the teams on the right side of it.

Method

What this is and what it isn't

Projection

Every drafted player is matched to a positional rank on the league's own 2026 board (218 ranked players; 164 of 168 non-defense picks matched directly, four cheap backup QBs assigned by hand). Ranks convert to season points through position-specific curves calibrated to this league's exact scoring — including the 0.2 points per rush attempt, which is worth about 50 points a year to a workhorse back, and the 5-point passing touchdown.

Simulation

  • 20,000 seasons; 14 regular-season weeks, then a 6-team bracket in weeks 15–17.
  • Weekly scores drawn per player with position-specific variance (QB tightest at 0.32 coefficient of variation, WR/TE widest at 0.55).
  • Injuries modelled as a weekly hazard by position, raised for players with nine or more years of experience and for high-volume backs, with duration drawn from an exponential.
  • Bye weeks assigned randomly per NFL team each season, so rosters stacked on one team take correlated hits.
  • Lineups filled optimally from whoever is available; unfilled slots score at streamer replacement level.
  • Both the head-to-head result and the weekly median match count, matching this league's league_average_match setting.

Auction value
