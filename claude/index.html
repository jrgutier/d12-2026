Where the spread actually is

Points per game from the best player at each position down to replacement level

Replacement level is not the same distance below the top at every position, and in this format the gap at quarterback is the smallest of the four. Thirty-two of the thirty-five quarterbacks drafted project as full-time starters, clustered between 15.2 and 25.4 points a game — so the twenty-fourth of them, the last one who starts in a given week, is only 4.8 points behind the best. A running back that far down costs you 12.6. That asymmetry is the single most important fact about this auction.

The market

Dollars paid vs. projected value, by position

Priced against replacement, the room put 22.5% of its money into a position worth 13.9% of the value in the player pool — overpaying for quarterbacks by 61%, the largest mispricing anywhere in the draft. Running backs and receivers came out close to fair. The position the room actually left on the table was tight end, bought at 61 cents on the dollar, and team defenses at 20 cents.

Does the answer hold?

Top three under five deliberate distortions of the projections

The fair objection to any weekly projection is that it regresses toward the mean — nobody forecasts four touchdowns, so the projected spread at quarterback is narrower than the spread that actually gets realised. The important test is what happens when that compression is undone. Stretching quarterback spread by 2.5× does not rescue the quarterback-heavy rosters: it makes the leader stronger, because it owns three startable quarterbacks of its own, and Joshin’ You Not still finishes twelfth. The answer is not an artifact of how flat the quarterback curve is.

Method

What this is and what it isn't

Projections

Every player's week-by-week 2026 projection comes from Sleeper's own API, sourced from Rotowire, and is scored with this league's exact settings — iterating the league's scoring keys against each projected stat line, so the 0.2 points per rush attempt and the 5-point passing touchdown apply directly rather than being approximated. All 180 drafted players resolved, team defenses included.

Real schedules, real part-seasons

  • A missing week in a projection is a real bye, recovered unanimously across all 32 NFL teams — so rosters stacked on one team take correlated hits in the weeks that actually matter.
  • Part-seasons are modelled as written: Kirk Cousins projects to start weeks 1–2 before Fernando Mendoza takes over, Josh Jacobs and Zach Charbonnet miss week 1, and Michael Penix has no projection at all.

Simulation

  • 20,000 seasons; 14 regular-season weeks, then a 6-team bracket in weeks 15–17.
  • Weekly scores drawn per player around the projection with position-specific variance — quarterback tightest, receiver and tight end widest.
  • Injuries as a weekly hazard by position, raised for players with nine or more years of experience and for high-volume backs, duration drawn from an exponential. Sleeper's projections carry no availability discount of their own, so this does not double-count.
  • Lineups filled optimally from whoever is available; unfilled slots score at streamer replacement level.
  • Both the head-to-head result and the weekly median match count, matching this league's league_average_match setting.

Auction value

Value above replacement against superflex baselines — QB24, RB30, WR42, TE14 — allocated across the $2,400 pool with $1 reserved per roster spot.

The honest caveat. This projects the preseason, not the season. It inherits every error in the underlying projections and has watched no football: no camp news, no depth-chart surprises, no in-season trades or waiver claims, all of which move a fourteen-week outcome more than anything on this page.

Weekly projections also understate upside by construction. The simulator adds symmetric variance around each forecast, which does not recreate the fatter right tail an elite player carries. If you think that tail is worth paying for, the quarterback market here looks less irrational than the numbers say — though the robustness check above suggests it would not change who wins.

And the spread is narrow: 23.7 points a game separate first from last across a full lineup, and the win ranges overlap heavily. Treat the ordering as a prior, not a result.
