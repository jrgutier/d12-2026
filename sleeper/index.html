Two Boards, One Draft

Re-running the season projection on Sleeper's own weekly numbers instead of the league board moves eleven of twelve teams and reverses the result. The team that was most likely to win the title is now last. Almost all of it traces to a single disagreement about one position.

Sleeper / Rotowire weekly projections — this page The league's 2026 ranking board — the first report

What changed

Rank under each projection source, 20,000 seasons each

Only one team holds its position at the top. Uncs Gone Wild is the sole roster that finishes in the top two under both sources — it never needed either source's opinion about quarterbacks to be right. Everything else moves, and Joshin’ You Not falls ten places, from a 24.1% title favorite to 0.0%.

One position explains it

Points per game from the best player to replacement level

The two sources are close to interchangeable at running back, receiver and tight end — within about a point of each other on how much the best player beats a replacement. At quarterback they are not close at all. The board says an elite quarterback is worth 17.5 points a game over the 24th-best; Sleeper says 4.8. In a league that starts 24 quarterbacks a week, that one number decides who wins.

Dollars paid vs. value derived from Sleeper's projections

Does it survive the obvious objection?

Top three under five distortions, including a deliberate de-regression of quarterback

The fair criticism of Sleeper's numbers is that weekly projections regress toward the mean: nobody forecasts four touchdowns, so the projected spread at quarterback is narrower than the spread that actually gets realised. So the important test is what happens when that compression is undone. Stretching quarterback spread by 2.5× — back to roughly the board's shape — does not rescue the quarterback-heavy teams. It makes the leader stronger, because it has three startable quarterbacks of its own, and Joshin’ You Not still finishes twelfth.

Method

What is different from the first report, and what isn't

What changed

  • Projections. Every player's week-by-week 2026 projection is pulled from Sleeper's own API (sourced from Rotowire) and scored with this league's exact settings — iterating the league's scoring keys against the projected stat line, so the 0.2 per rush attempt and the 5-point passing touchdown are applied directly rather than approximated by a curve. All 180 drafted players resolved.
  • Real bye weeks. A missing week in the projection is a real bye, recovered unanimously for all 32 NFL teams. The first report assigned byes at random.
  • Real part-seasons. Kirk Cousins is projected to start weeks 1–2 before Fernando Mendoza takes over; Josh Jacobs and Zach Charbonnet miss week 1; Michael Penix has no projection at all. Those are modelled as written rather than smoothed away.

What deliberately did not change

Same simulator, same 20,000 seasons, same weekly variance by position, same injury hazards, same lineup optimiser, same schedule and median-match scoring, same replacement logic and the same value-above-replacement baselines (QB24, RB30, WR42, TE14). Only the projection source moved, so the difference between the two reports is attributable to it and nothing else.

Availability

Sleeper's projections carry gp: 1.0 for every player, so they do not discount for injury risk — the simulator's own hazard model is not double-counting. The 38 "Questionable" tags attached to the draft picks are from draft day in August and are stale; the projections are current.

Which report is right? Neither, on its own. They are two defensible answers to one honest question: how much does an elite quarterback really beat a freely available one in a superflex? Everything else the two sources agree on.

Sleeper's answer is the safer one for expected value, because projected spread is what you can actually buy; realised spread includes luck that is not for sale. But it is compressed by regression, and it understates upside — the simulator adds symmetric weekly variance, which does not recreate the fatter right tail an elite quarterback has. The truth is very likely between the two, which is why the roster that ranks near the top of both is the most interesting one on the page.

And the standing caveat holds: this projects the preseason, not the season. Nothing here has watched a snap.
